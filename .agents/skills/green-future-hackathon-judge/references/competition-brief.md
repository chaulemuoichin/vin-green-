# Asian Hackathon for Green Future 2026 Brief

This reference condenses the two competition PDFs provided in the workspace.

## Competition

Asian Hackathon for Green Future 2026 is a student and master's student hackathon for technology solutions serving a sustainable future. It is organised by Foundation for Green Future, VinUni, and VinTechTalent.

Total prize pool: USD 24,000.

Prize structure:

- First prize: USD 8,000.
- Second prize: USD 5,000.
- Two third prizes: USD 3,000 each.
- Five consolation prizes: USD 1,000 each.

## Eligible Teams

- Students in full-time undergraduate or master's programmes at universities across Asia.
- Maximum 4 members per team.
- One team leader handles registration and communication.
- Members cannot be changed after registration except force majeure cases approved by the organiser.

## Challenge Areas

Each team must select one:

1. Renewable Energy and Low-Carbon Mobility.
2. Urban Air Quality and Climate Resilience.
3. Water Resources and Climate-Resilient Agriculture.

## Competition Rounds

Preliminary round:

- Dates: 06 Apr 2026 to 17 May 2026.
- Registration via organiser's online form.
- Required proposal: maximum 3 A4 pages, around 1,500 words.
- Required video: about 3 minutes introducing the team and idea.
- Top 30 teams are selected.
- Top 30 announcement expected: 01 Jun 2026.

Online training:

- Dates: 02 Jun 2026 to 22 Jun 2026.
- Top 30 teams receive training and mentoring.

In-person hackathon/final/award at VinUni:

- Expected dates: 02 Jul 2026 to 05 Jul 2026.
- Top 30 invited to Hanoi, Vietnam.
- 24-hour in-person hackathon at VinUni.
- Teams present using PowerPoint, Canva, or equivalent.
- Judges select Top 9 for the final round.

Language:

- English is used in registration and throughout the competition.

## Preliminary Proposal Requirements

Submission formats:

- PDF, DOCX, or presentation files such as PPT/PPTX/Canva.

File naming:

- `[TeamName]_[ProjectName]_PreliminaryRound.pdf`

Recommended length:

- Maximum 3 A4 pages.
- Approximately 1,500 words.

The proposal should be concise, well-structured, evidence-grounded, and creative.

## Recommended Proposal Structure

1. Project Overview
   - Project title.
   - One challenge area.
   - Solution category: mobile app/web app, software/platform, hardware/IoT device, integrated hardware+software, or other.
   - 4-6 sentence project summary covering problem, solution, key technologies, and target beneficiaries.

2. Context and Problem Statement
   - Clear and specific environmental issue.
   - Scale and affected stakeholders.
   - Negative impacts and consequences.
   - Context, magnitude, geography, urgency, and involved groups.

3. Existing Solutions and Gap Analysis
   - Similar market/current solutions.
   - Strengths of existing solutions.
   - Limitations, gaps, unresolved issues, and barriers to adoption or scalability.

4. Proposed Solution and Core Features
   - How the solution addresses the problem.
   - 3-5 demonstrable prototype features with brief function descriptions.

5. Innovation and Competitive Advantage
   - Breakthrough or innovative aspects.
   - Differentiation from competitors.
   - Feasibility and development plan.
   - Hackathon phase plan, post-hackathon roadmap, and real-world implementation feasibility.

6. Target Groups and Potential Impact
   - Clearly defined end users.
   - Environmental, economic, social, or community benefits if successful.

7. Description of Technologies Applied
   - Technologies, programming languages, frameworks, libraries, algorithms.
   - System architecture and technical approach.
   - Data flows, key components, and interactions.
   - Data sources and infrastructure requirements.

8. References
   - Academic papers, market research, websites, books, or other sources.
   - Cite clearly.

## What Final/Hackathon Judges Expect

During the in-person hackathon and final rounds, teams need to demonstrate:

- Innovative problem and solution.
- Technical approach or deployment model.
- Impact and feasibility.
- A prototype or concrete demonstration, not just a concept.

## Judge Interpretation

For this skill, interpret "good" as:

- Clear problem within one challenge area.
- Evidence-grounded and specific.
- Strong gap analysis.
- Technically plausible architecture.
- Prototype demonstrable in 24 hours.
- Quantified impact with units and assumptions.
- Differentiated from existing solutions.
- Strong English communication and concise storytelling.
